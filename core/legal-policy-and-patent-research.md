# Legal, Policy, and Patent Research

## Purpose
Research legal rules, cases, regulation, policy, and patent status with jurisdiction/version/effective-date discipline.

## Legal rule frame
Resolve:
`jurisdiction -> authority level -> instrument/case -> version/effective date -> definitions -> amendments/commencement -> procedural posture -> controlling/persuasive status`.

Use official legislation, court/judiciary records, regulators/gazettes, parliamentary records, and official patent registers for the facts they own. Commentary is useful for discovery/context, not a substitute for controlling text.

## Historical law
When asking what applied on a past date, retrieve the text/status in force then, not merely today's consolidated version. Check commencement, transitional provisions, amendments, repeals, appeals, and later overruling where relevant.

## Cases
Separate holding from dicta, facts, procedural posture, standard of review, jurisdiction/court hierarchy, subsequent treatment, and whether the decision is final/reported/appealed.

## Policy/government
Distinguish enacted law, regulation, binding guidance, draft rule, consultation, bill, political statement, and implementation practice.

## Patents
For patent/prior-art work track:
- family/priority dates;
- jurisdiction/application/publication/grant identifiers;
- claims and prosecution history where material;
- citations/prior art and earliest public disclosure;
- continuations/divisionals/oppositions/reexaminations;
- renewal/fee/status/expiry/lapse from official registers.

A search engine's "active" label is discovery evidence; verify legal status in the relevant official register.

## Output
Avoid overstating legal certainty. State jurisdiction/effective date and whether the result is research synthesis rather than professional legal advice when that distinction matters.
