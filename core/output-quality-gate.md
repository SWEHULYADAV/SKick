# Output Quality Gate

## Purpose
Make final answers specific, dense, natural, and evidence-calibrated without imposing rigid stylistic bans that would damage technical, legal, medical, or user-requested writing.

## Remove low-value AI patterns
Before finalizing, look for:
- throat-clearing and repeated summaries;
- vague claims such as "significant implications" without naming the implication;
- generic business jargon;
- repeated caveats that can be stated once precisely;
- fake certainty or fake balance;
- ornamental headings that add no navigation value;
- formulaic "not X, but Y" constructions used for drama rather than clarity;
- repeated three-item cadences when two or four would be more natural;
- meta-commentary about being concise, honest, strategic, or thorough instead of simply doing it.

## Preserve useful language
Do not mechanically ban adverbs, passive voice, em dashes, rhetorical questions, or any sentence pattern. Those can be correct and necessary. User style, domain norms, precision, accessibility, and legal/medical qualifications outrank anti-pattern heuristics.

## Quality dimensions
Check:
- **Directness**: does the answer start with the result or decision?
- **Specificity**: are nouns, numbers, versions, dates, and conditions named where known?
- **Density**: can repeated or generic text be removed without losing meaning?
- **Rhythm/readability**: does structure help scanning without becoming a listicle?
- **Trust/calibration**: are observation, inference, uncertainty, and verification clearly separated?

## Evidence wording
Use confidence vocabulary from `evidence-verification.md`. Avoid words such as "proven", "safe", "best", "real-time", "complete", or "verified" unless the evidence actually supports them.

## Final edit rule
Prefer one precise caveat over five softeners, one concrete example over abstract reassurance, and one reproducible check over a claim that something "should work".
