# Biomedical and Health Research

## Purpose
Research biomedical mechanisms, clinical evidence, drugs, diagnostics, epidemiology, and public-health questions with source hierarchy and evidence-level discipline.

## Route evidence by claim
Use `research-source-router.md` and `empirical-research-methodology.md`.

Typical claim owners:
- trial registration/status/results -> ClinicalTrials.gov or jurisdictional registry;
- US drug labeling -> DailyMed/FDA label and label history;
- approvals/safety communications/recalls -> relevant regulator;
- biomedical literature -> PubMed/publisher plus corrections/retractions;
- preprints -> medRxiv/bioRxiv, explicitly non-peer-reviewed until verified otherwise;
- compound/target/bioactivity -> PubChem/ChEMBL/Open Targets with assay/release context;
- disease classification -> exact WHO ICD revision;
- public-health guidance/statistics -> WHO/CDC/national authority for the relevant jurisdiction/date.

## Mechanism vs clinical effect
A plausible molecular/pathogen mechanism does not prove patient benefit. Separate:
`mechanistic evidence -> preclinical -> early human/biomarker -> controlled clinical outcome -> real-world effectiveness -> safety`.

## Benefit/risk
For interventions, inspect dose/route/population, comparator, endpoints, absolute as well as relative effects where possible, adverse events, contraindications/interactions, follow-up duration, subgroup evidence, and regulatory status.

## Safety signals
Spontaneous adverse-event reports can generate signals but do not alone establish incidence or causation. Verify with stronger epidemiologic/clinical/regulatory evidence when available.

## High-stakes discipline
Preserve uncertainty, population/jurisdiction, and date. Distinguish general research synthesis from individualized diagnosis or treatment instructions.
